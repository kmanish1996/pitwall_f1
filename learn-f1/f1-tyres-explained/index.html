F1 tyres explained: soft, medium, hard, intermediate and wet (2026)

Learn F1 · Tyres · Open the interactive version

Tyres are the only part of the car touching the track, and managing them decides races.

Soft

Red stripe

The fastest tyre over one lap and the quickest to warm up, which makes it the qualifying favourite. It wears out fastest, so race stints on it are short.

Medium

Yellow stripe

The all-rounder. A little slower than the soft but lasts longer, so it's often the race-start tyre or the bridge in a one-stop strategy.

Hard

White stripe

The most durable dry tyre and the slowest to come up to temperature. Teams use it for long stints, and on cold days drivers can struggle to make it work.

Intermediate

Green stripe

For a damp or drying track. Shallow grooves clear standing water, but on a dry track the tread overheats and wears out very quickly.

Full wet

Blue stripe

For heavy rain. Deep grooves channel huge amounts of water out from under the tyre to stop the car aquaplaning. It's only needed when there's a lot of standing water.

How tyres work in 2026

  • Five dry compounds, C1 to C5. C1 is the hardest and C5 the softest; the C6 used in 2025 was dropped.
  • Three per weekend. Pirelli picks three: the hardest is called the Hard (white), the middle the Medium (yellow), the softest the Soft (red).
  • Two for the rain: Intermediates (green) and full Wets (blue).
  • Two compounds in a dry race. Every driver must use at least two different dry compounds, so everyone pits at least once.
  • Narrower for 2026. Still 18-inch wheels, but the fronts are 25 mm narrower and the rears 30 mm narrower.

Tyre talk

Degradation ("deg")
The tyre slowly losing grip as it wears and heats up, so lap times drop off.
Graining
Little rolls of rubber tear off and stick to the surface, making the tyre slide until they wear away.
Blistering
The rubber gets so hot underneath that pockets bubble up and break off.
Undercut
Pitting before a rival so your fresh tyres let you set faster laps and jump ahead when they stop.
Overcut
Staying out longer than a rival and using clear track to gain time instead.
Marbles
Bits of worn rubber that collect off the racing line and feel like driving on ball bearings.

Questions and answers

What are the different F1 tyres?

Three dry tyres each weekend, Soft (red), Medium (yellow) and Hard (white), chosen from five compounds C1 to C5, plus Intermediates (green) and full Wets (blue) for rain.

Why do F1 cars have to change tyres?

In a dry race every driver must use at least two different dry compounds, and tyres lose grip as they wear, so drivers make at least one pit stop.

What is the undercut in F1?

Pitting before a rival so your fresh tyres let you set faster laps and jump ahead when they stop.

What is tyre degradation in F1?

The tyre slowly losing grip as it wears and heats up, so lap times drop off.
