F1 race weekend explained: practice, qualifying, sprint and the points system

Learn F1 · Race weekend & points · Open the interactive version

A Grand Prix weekend runs from Friday to Sunday. Most weekends have three practice sessions, qualifying on Saturday and the race on Sunday. Six weekends in 2026 are sprint weekends, with Sprint Qualifying on Friday and a short Sprint race on Saturday.

Normal weekend

Friday: Practice 1, Practice 2. Saturday: Practice 3, Qualifying. Sunday: Grand Prix.

Sprint weekend

Friday: Practice 1, Sprint Qualifying. Saturday: Sprint, Qualifying. Sunday: Grand Prix.

Every session explained

Practice 1

Friday · every weekend · 60 min

The first run on track. Teams check the car works, try new parts and settings, and drivers relearn the track as the surface gathers rubber and gets faster. Teams also have to give young rookie drivers a few FP1 sessions every season.

Practice 2

Friday · normal weekends · 60 min

Usually the most important practice. Teams do long runs with lots of fuel to see how quickly each tyre wears out over a race distance, which shapes Sunday's strategy. They also try a few fast laps on low fuel.

Practice 3

Saturday · normal weekends · 60 min

The final hour of practice before qualifying. Teams fine-tune the set-up and drivers do qualifying simulations: one flat-out lap on fresh soft tyres and low fuel, to be ready for the afternoon.

Sprint Qualifying

Friday · sprint weekends · SQ1 12 · SQ2 10 · SQ3 8 min

A shorter version of qualifying that sets the grid for Saturday's Sprint. It works the same way: the slowest six drop out in SQ1, six more in SQ2, and the top ten fight for Sprint pole in SQ3.

Sprint

Saturday · sprint weekends · ~100 km · ~30 min

A short, flat-out race of about 100 km, roughly a third of a Grand Prix, with no compulsory pit stop. The top eight score points, from 8 for the winner down to 1 for eighth.

Qualifying

Saturday · every weekend · Q1 18 · Q2 15 · Q3 12 min

The knockout that sets Sunday's grid. Everyone runs in Q1 and the slowest six are out; another six go in Q2; the top ten battle for pole position in Q3. A driver slower than 107% of the fastest Q1 time may not be allowed to start.

The Grand Prix

Sunday · every weekend · ~305 km · max 2 hours

After a formation lap, five red lights come on one by one and then go out: lights out and away we go. The race runs for about 305 km (Monaco is shorter), with a two-hour limit. In a dry race every driver must use two different tyre compounds, so everyone pits at least once. The top ten score points.

The F1 points system

A perfect weekend is worth 25 points, or 33 on a sprint weekend. There has been no bonus point for the fastest lap since 2025. Both of a team's drivers score for the Constructors' Championship, and ties are broken by countback: most wins, then most second places, and so on.

Questions and answers

How many points do you get for winning an F1 race?

25 points. The top ten score 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and 1.

How many points is an F1 sprint worth?

The top eight in a Sprint score 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 points.

How does F1 qualifying work?

It's a knockout in three parts. In Q1 (18 minutes) the slowest six of the 22 cars are eliminated, in Q2 (15 minutes) another six go, and the top ten fight for pole position in Q3 (12 minutes).

What is a sprint race in F1?

A short race of about 100 km on Saturday of a sprint weekend, roughly a third of a Grand Prix, with no compulsory pit stop. Its grid is set by Sprint Qualifying on Friday.

How long is an F1 race?

About 305 km (Monaco is shorter), usually around 90 minutes, with a two-hour limit.

Is there a point for the fastest lap?

Not any more. The bonus point for the fastest lap was dropped from 2025.
