How the F1 start works

At the start of a Grand Prix, five pairs of red lights come on one after another, a second apart. After all five are lit there's a short, random pause, then they all go out together. That's the signal to go. React too early and it's a jump start; the stewards hand out a penalty.

The famous 0.201s

At the 2017 Austrian Grand Prix, Valtteri Bottas got away so fast that rivals thought he had jumped the start. The FIA looked at the data, found he had reacted in 0.201 seconds, and let his win stand. His time was measured from lights out to the car moving; yours here is from lights out to your tap.
