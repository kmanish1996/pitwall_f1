Albert Park Circuit

Melbourne · Round 1 of the 2026 season · Australian Grand Prix 2026

Albert Park Circuit is a 5.278 km Formula 1 circuit in Melbourne with 14 corners, first used for a world championship Grand Prix in 1996. George Russell won the 2026 Australian Grand Prix here. George Russell's 2026 pole lap of 1:18.518 peaked at 327 km/h.

Interesting facts

  • A race track in a city park. The circuit runs on Albert Park's everyday roads and loops around Albert Park Lake. It's a temporary street circuit, built up for race week.
  • The season opener's home since 1996, when the Australian Grand Prix moved to Melbourne from Adelaide.
  • Rebuilt for speed in 2022. The old Turn 9–10 chicane was removed and Turn 6 widened by more than 7.5 m, worth about 70 km/h there. The changes were expected to cut lap times by around five seconds, and the corner count dropped from 16 to 14.
  • Almost pancake flat. On the pole lap, the highest and lowest points of the track are only 2.4 m apart, so the 3D view exaggerates the height to make it visible.
  • Michael Schumacher won here four times, more than any other driver.

Questions and answers

How long is Albert Park Circuit?

Albert Park Circuit is 5.278 km long, with 14 corners. The 2026 Australian Grand Prix runs over 58 laps, a race distance of 306.124 km.

What is the lap record at Albert Park Circuit?

The race lap record is 1:19.813, set by Charles Leclerc (Ferrari) in 2024.

Who was on pole for the 2026 Australian Grand Prix?

George Russell (Mercedes) took pole with a 1:18.518. On that lap the car reached 327 km/h and averaged 241 km/h.

Who won the 2026 Australian Grand Prix?

George Russell won the 2026 Australian Grand Prix, in a race time of 1:23:06.801.

When was the first Formula 1 race at Albert Park Circuit?

1996.
