Circuit Gilles Villeneuve

Montréal · Round 5 of the 2026 season · Canadian Grand Prix 2026

Circuit Gilles Villeneuve is a 4.361 km Formula 1 circuit in Montréal with 14 corners, first used for a world championship Grand Prix in 1978. Kimi Antonelli won the 2026 Canadian Grand Prix here. George Russell's 2026 pole lap of 1:12.578 peaked at 332 km/h.

Interesting facts

  • On an island. The circuit is on Île Notre-Dame, a man-made island in the St Lawrence River built for the Expo 67 world's fair.
  • The Wall of Champions. The wall at the exit of the final chicane got its name in 1999, when Damon Hill, Michael Schumacher and Jacques Villeneuve all crashed into it.
  • Named after Gilles Villeneuve, who won the first Grand Prix held here in 1978.
  • Stop, go, stop, go. Long straights into slow chicanes make it hard on brakes: the slowest point of the pole lap was 60 km/h.

Questions and answers

How long is Circuit Gilles Villeneuve?

Circuit Gilles Villeneuve is 4.361 km long, with 14 corners. The 2026 Canadian Grand Prix runs over 70 laps, a race distance of 305.270 km.

What is the lap record at Circuit Gilles Villeneuve?

The race lap record is 1:13.078, set by Valtteri Bottas (Mercedes) in 2019.

Who was on pole for the 2026 Canadian Grand Prix?

George Russell (Mercedes) took pole with a 1:12.578. On that lap the car reached 332 km/h and averaged 214 km/h.

Who won the 2026 Canadian Grand Prix?

Kimi Antonelli won the 2026 Canadian Grand Prix, in a race time of 1:28:15.758.

When was the first Formula 1 race at Circuit Gilles Villeneuve?

1978.
