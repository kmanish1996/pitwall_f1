Meet the drivers and live the weekend

Where fans actually get close to the stars.

Thursday

Autograph sessions

Most circuits hold a driver signing on Thursday, often at the Fan Zone stage. Places are limited and are often handed out by wristband, so check the circuit app and arrive early.

Thursday · Friday

Pit lane walk

Walk past the garages and watch crews practise pit stops. Some circuits include it with weekend tickets. Official F1 Experiences packages run quieter walks while the track is closed to the public.

Friday · Saturday

Fan Zone stage

Drivers and team bosses appear for interviews and fan forums, often straight after qualifying. Arrive early for a spot near the stage.

Sunday

Drivers' parade

Before the race, drivers ride a lap of the circuit and wave to every stand. Seats at the front of a grandstand get the closest look.

After the race

Track invasion

At several circuits, including Monza, marshals open the track after the chequered flag so fans can gather under the podium.

Any day

Packages and hospitality

F1 Experiences packages add guided track tours, pit lane walks and trophy photos. Paddock Club and team hospitality cost thousands per person and put you above the pit lane.

        Wanna learn about F1?

        New to Formula 1, or want to sound smart on race day? Here's what F1 is, how a race weekend and the championships work, the track talk, tyres, steering wheel, tech and people, all explained with moving pictures.

        The pinnacle of motorsport

        Engineering at the limit, at 300 km/h

        Formula 1 is the world's fastest racing championship. Eleven teams build their own cars from scratch, pushing engineering, aerodynamics, materials and software as far as the rules allow, and then 22 of the best drivers on the planet race them around the world.

        It's a sport, a technology race and a travelling show all at once: the cars change every few weeks, the season visits five continents, and the margin between winning and losing is often a few hundredths of a second.

        A brief history

          A Grand Prix weekend runs from Friday to Sunday. There are two formats: the normal weekend and the sprint weekend, which has an extra short race on Saturday. Tap a session to jump to it.

          Points: what a weekend is worth

          Grand Prix · top 10 score
          Sprint · top 8 score
          • A perfect weekend is worth 25 points, or 33 on a sprint weekend (8 for the Sprint win plus 25 for the Grand Prix).
          • No bonus for the fastest lap since 2025. Points go to finishing positions only.
          • Both of a team's drivers score for the Constructors' Championship, so a team can earn up to 43 points in a Grand Prix (25 + 18).
          • Ties are broken by countback: whoever has more wins is ahead; if that's level, more second places, and so on.
          • A shortened race (for example, stopped early by rain) can pay reduced points, depending on how much of it was completed.
          • Points decide both world championships: see the Trophies tab.

          Two titles are up for grabs every season: the World Drivers' Championship for the driver with the most points, and the World Constructors' Championship for the team. Both are presented at the FIA's prize-giving at the end of the year.

          World Drivers' Championship

          The big one. Every point a driver scores in Grands Prix and Sprints counts, and whoever has the most at the end of the season is Formula 1 World Champion. It can be settled at the final race, or with several rounds to spare.

          Most titles
          Since 1958

          World Constructors' Championship

          The teams' title. Both drivers' points are added together, so a team needs two strong drivers. For the teams it's worth more than glory: the final order decides each team's share of the sport's prize money.

          How tyres work in 2026
          • Five dry compounds, C1 to C5. C1 is the hardest and C5 the softest. The C6 used in 2025 was dropped.
          • Three per weekend. Pirelli picks three of them for each race. The hardest of the three is called the Hard (white), the middle one the Medium (yellow) and the softest the Soft (red). So a "Soft" in Monaco can be a different rubber from a "Soft" in Bahrain.
          • Plus two for the rain: Intermediates (green) and full Wets (blue).
          • Two compounds in a dry race. Each driver has to use at least two different dry compounds, which is why everyone makes at least one pit stop.
          • Narrower for 2026. Still on 18-inch wheels, but the fronts are 25 mm narrower, the rears 30 mm narrower, and the diameter drops from 720 mm to about 705–710 mm.
          Tyre talk
          Degradation ("deg")
          The tyre slowly losing grip as it wears and heats up, so lap times drop off.
          Graining
          Little rolls of rubber tear off and stick to the surface, making the tyre slide until they wear away.
          Blistering
          The rubber gets so hot underneath that pockets bubble up and break off.
          Undercut
          Pitting before a rival so your fresh tyres let you set faster laps and jump ahead when they stop.
          Overcut
          Staying out longer than a rival and using clear track to gain time instead.
          Marbles
          Bits of worn rubber that collect off the racing line and feel like driving on ball bearings.
